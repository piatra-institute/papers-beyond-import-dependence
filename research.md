## The measured anchors (T1, all verified this session)

**Lithography and its optics.** ASML's 2025 annual report records approximately 5,100 suppliers; the company shipped 48 extreme ultraviolet systems in 2025, and deep ultraviolet system sales fell from 374 units in 2024 to 279 in 2025. ZEISS states that it exclusively supplies the optics for these machines: the High-NA projection optics contain more than 40,000 parts and weigh around twelve tons, the illumination system more than 25,000 parts and over six tons, the collaborating network numbers more than 1,200 members, and a mirror takes around a year to manufacture. The seed's further claim of 208 metrology and inspection systems could not be confirmed and is **dropped**.

**The embargoed node.** Reuters, June 19, 2026: ASML stated it had never shipped an extreme ultraviolet machine to China, nor any component, module, or equipment specially designed for one, after a report that United States officials were concerned such a tool may have reached the country; an internal presentation cited by the reporting puts 314 such systems operating worldwide with none in China. This is the paper's central inversion in one sentence: recorded imports zero, exposure extreme.

**Chinese lithography status.** Reuters, July 28, 2026: China began mass production of domestically developed immersion deep ultraviolet tools, led by a state-owned firm that absorbed teams from several lithography startups, targeting roughly 5 machines in 2026 and about 20 in 2027, with deliveries planned to leading domestic chipmakers, while some critical components continue to be imported from Japan and the machines remain far from the incumbent's models. Reuters, March 5, 2026: senior Chinese semiconductor executives called publicly for coordinated national effort to build "China's ASML," naming electronic design automation software, silicon wafers, and electronic gases as bottlenecks alongside lithography, and describing progress in extreme ultraviolet light sources, wafer stages, and optical systems with **integration into a complete system unresolved**. Prototype is not production; production of one generation is not parity with a moving frontier.

**The controls.** Bureau of Industry and Security, December 2, 2024: new controls on 24 types of semiconductor manufacturing equipment and 3 types of software tools for developing or producing chips, controls on high-bandwidth memory, and 140 additions to the Entity List. The controlled surface is the policymaker's own estimate of the consequential dependency graph, and it is far wider than one machine.

**Clean-energy concentration.** International Energy Agency, Energy Technology Perspectives 2026: China accounts for around 85 percent of solar and 80 percent of lithium-ion battery supply-chain production capacity, with higher shares for photovoltaic wafers at 95 percent and anode materials at 97 percent; it processes over 70 percent of lithium, cobalt, graphite, and rare earths, and supplies 85 percent of natural graphite. Each month of an interruption in battery supply-chain exports from the largest supplier would cost about 17 billion United States dollars in lost output at electric-car plants elsewhere, with almost two-thirds of the losses in the European Union. The report's own weakest-link finding: each supply chain contains at least one step where production outside the largest exporter covers less than one quarter of demand. Fires at Chinese polysilicon plants in 2020 and 2022 caused major global price spikes and supply bottlenecks.

**The counter-anchor that disciplines the European story.** International Energy Agency commentary on inverter supply chains and cybersecurity: Europe had capacity to manufacture around 95 GW of inverters per year in 2025 against total shipments of around 90 GW across all categories; the price premium for European Union shipments over Chinese ones was around 13 United States dollars per kilowatt for utility-scale systems, roughly 75 percent higher, implying a project cost premium of just under 2 percent, with 3 to 4 percent for residential string inverters and around 20 percent for hybrid and microinverters. A market share of imports is not the same fact as an absent capability.

**Trade-based vulnerability, measured.** Connell Garcia and Ho (2025), Intereconomics 60(1), presenting the European Commission's External Vulnerability Index on 2022 BACI data: semiconductors, European Union 0.22, United States 0.19, China 0.17; net-zero technologies 0.18, 0.26, 0.10; raw materials 0.28, 0.32, 0.24; all industrial products 0.22, 0.28, 0.13. On this measure the European Union is more exposed than China and less exposed than the United States overall, exceeding the United States only in semiconductors. The paper uses this against the simple declinist reading and as the concrete instrument whose blind spots the first mechanism formalizes.

**Production closure without knowledge loss.** Government Accountability Office, GAO-26-107882 (December 2025): the United States share of global semiconductor manufacturing capacity fell from 37 percent in 1990 to 10 percent in 2022, and as of 2022 the country produced no leading-edge logic chips at commercial scale and only 3 percent of the two most common memory types. Department of Energy, grid supply chain: lead times of two or more years for critical equipment and transformer price increases of four to nine times over five years for some categories. This is the reconstitution-time condition rather than the epistemic one.

**The cost of the obvious remedy.** Organisation for Economic Co-operation and Development, Supply Chain Resilience Review (June 2025): modelled relocalisation could reduce global trade by over 18 percent and global real GDP by more than 5 percent, and increased GDP volatility in more than half of the economies modelled. Resilience through relocalisation is not merely expensive; in the majority of modelled cases it fails at the thing it is bought for.

**Subsystem dependence inside an integrated program.** The C919 flies with the CFM International LEAP-1C, from the General Electric and Safran joint venture. In May 2025 the United States Department of Commerce suspended export licences covering aircraft engines and related technology to the airframer; the suspension was lifted in July 2025. The domestic alternative, the CJ-1000A, is projected for certification around 2027 and service around 2030. The episode is a natural experiment: the dependency was invisible in ordinary statistics, became a program-level risk within weeks of a licence decision, and reverted, leaving the capability position unchanged.

**Capability ladders.** World Health Organization, Bulletin (Dong and Mirza, 2016, 94: 71–72): in most of sub-Saharan Africa, imports account for as much as 70 to 90 percent of medicines consumed, and local production requires regulatory systems, quality control, and technology transfer rather than plants alone. United Nations Industrial Development Organization, Industrial Development Report 2026 and its Latin America and Caribbean regional report (July 2026): persistent low growth, weak productivity, and premature deindustrialization, with upgrading opportunities named by sector. Both are used for the operational-maintenance-reproductive-developmental ladder rather than for regional scores.

**China's own program.** Recommendations of the Central Committee for the 15th Five-Year Plan (October 2025): "decisive breakthroughs" in core technologies "across entire chains" in integrated circuits, industrial machine tools, high-end equipment, basic software, advanced materials, and biomanufacturing. Read as a policy statement it is an explicit program for reducing black-box depth, and its stated unit is the chain rather than the product.

## The conceptual core (T1)

- **Brusoni, Prencipe and Pavitt (2001)**, Administrative Science Quarterly 46: 597–621 (10.2307/3094825): why firms know more than they make; the knowledge boundary is not the production boundary.
- **Kapoor and Adner (2012)**, Organization Science 23: 1227–1248 (10.1287/orsc.1110.0686): the same distinction tested against performance under technological change.
- **Cohen and Levinthal (1990)**, Administrative Science Quarterly 35: 128–152 (10.2307/2393553): absorptive capacity; prior related knowledge as the condition for using external knowledge, which is why the sign of an import is conditional.
- **Gereffi, Humphrey and Sturgeon (2005)**, Review of International Political Economy 12: 78–104 (10.1080/09692290500049805): value-chain governance from complexity, codifiability, and supplier capability. **Sturgeon (2002)**, Industrial and Corporate Change 11: 451–496 (10.1093/icc/11.3.451): modular production networks and the delinking of production from innovation.
- **Henderson and Clark (1990)**, Administrative Science Quarterly 35: 9–30 (10.2307/2393549): architectural knowledge as a separately losable thing.
- **Teece (1986)**, Research Policy 15: 285–305 (10.1016/0048-7333(86)90027-2): complementary assets; holding the design without the production system loses the returns and eventually the design.
- **Baldwin and Clark (2000)**, *Design Rules* (10.7551/mitpress/2366.001.0001): modularity as option value; the paper's productive option value is its industrial dual.
- **Polanyi (1966)**, *The Tacit Dimension*; **Nelson and Winter (1982)**, *An Evolutionary Theory of Economic Change*: the part of a capability that no document carries.
- **Pisano and Shih (2012)**, *Producing Prosperity* (Harvard Business Review Press): the industrial commons and its dissolution when production leaves; the mechanism behind the model's scale cliff.
- **Lall (1992)**, World Development 20: 165–186 (10.1016/0305-750x(92)90097-f); **Bell and Pavitt (1993)**, Industrial and Corporate Change 2: 157–210 (10.1093/icc/2.2.157): technological capability ladders; operating a plant is not reproducing it.
- **Hidalgo and Hausmann (2009)**, PNAS 106: 10570–10575 (10.1073/pnas.0900943106): capabilities as the latent structure behind what a place makes.
- **Acemoglu, Carvalho, Ozdaglar and Tahbaz-Salehi (2012)**, Econometrica 80: 1977–2016 (10.3982/ECTA9623); **Barrot and Sauvagnat (2016)**, Quarterly Journal of Economics 131: 1543–1592 (10.1093/qje/qjw018); **Carvalho, Nirei, Saito and Tahbaz-Salehi (2021)**, Quarterly Journal of Economics 136: 1255–1321 (10.1093/qje/qjaa044): network propagation, input specificity, and the measured earthquake; the empirical warrant for criticality that money weights miss.
- **Farrell and Newman (2019)**, International Security 44: 42–79 (10.1162/isec_a_00351): weaponized interdependence; the access term is political.

## Seed corrections

- The seed's 208 metrology and inspection systems: unconfirmed, **dropped**; 48 extreme ultraviolet and 279 deep ultraviolet retained with the 374 comparison.
- The seed's Chinese extreme ultraviolet prototype timeline ("2028 official target, 2030 more likely"): traced only to unnamed sources in reporting the paper could not confirm at first hand, so the paper carries the March 2026 executives' statement and the July 2026 deep ultraviolet production report instead, both of which are attributable.
- The seed's 100,000 components and 5,000 suppliers from a Chinese journal article: the supplier figure is verified from the manufacturer's own annual report as approximately 5,100, so the paper cites the primary record; the component count is dropped.
- The seed's regional score table is a set of priors, and the paper carries it as such or not at all; no regional scores are asserted as findings.
- The seed's proposed empirical design (60 to 120 nodes per stack, paid databases, panel validation against eight historical shocks) is a research programme rather than a result. The paper states it as the programme its framework implies, and builds instead the three mechanisms that can be established from first principles: which index recovers ground truth, when imports build or erode capability, and how topology decides policy.
- The seed's electricity-price causal chain is retained only in its cautious form, and the paper makes no claim that black-box burden explains any observed price.
